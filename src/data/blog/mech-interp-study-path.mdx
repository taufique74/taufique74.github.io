<h2 id="start-here">Neel's 3-Stage Learning Path</h2>

Mechanistic interpretability is learnable on your own with short feedback loops
and modest compute. Don't over-prepare — get your hands dirty early.

<div class="trio not-prose">
  <div class="mini">
    <div class="num">Stage 1</div>
    <div class="lbl">≤ 1 month · basics</div>
    <p>
      Implement a transformer from scratch (ARENA), learn the core MI
      techniques, use TransformerLens, read one paper deeply.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="mini">
    <div class="num">Stage 2</div>
    <div class="lbl">1–5 day mini-projects</div>
    <p>
      Practice exploration and understanding with fast feedback loops. Quantity
      over polish.
    </p>
  </div>
  <div class="mini">
    <div class="num">Stage 3</div>
    <div class="lbl">1–2 week sprints</div>
    <p>
      Full research projects — continue your best mini-projects into
      publishable outputs.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

<p class="kicker">04 · Research Process</p>

<h2 id="research-craft">How to Do Good Research</h2>

Neel Nanda's blog posts on the craft itself — the meta-skills that separate
productive researchers from busy ones. Eight explainers in the local library
(`explainers/research_process/`):

- **Research Stages: Explore, Understand, Distill** — being in the wrong mode
  is the most common failure. _Must read._
- **Understanding and Cultivating Research Taste** — intuition + frameworks +
  strategy, built by prediction and post-mortems. _Must read._
- **Key Research Mindsets** — truth-seeking, prioritisation, moving fast.
  _Must read._
- **A Pragmatic Vision for Interpretability** — the field's pivot from

  SAE probes on downstream tasks.
- **Interpretability Dreams** — Olah's long-term vision: feature families,
