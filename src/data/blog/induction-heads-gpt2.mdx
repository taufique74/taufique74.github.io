An **induction head** implements a very small algorithm: given a sequence that has
partially repeated itself, look back to the previous occurrence of the current
token and attend to whatever came _after_ it. It is the mechanism behind a large
chunk of in-context learning, and it is the first non-trivial circuit most people
find when they start poking at a transformer.

The claim I want to check is narrow and falsifiable:

> In GPT-2 small, the heads with the highest induction score are L5H5, L5H1, and
> L6H9 — and they score far above the average head.

## The probe

Feed the model `[BOS] r₁ r₂ … r₁₅ r₁ r₂ … r₁₅`, where `r` is a random token
sequence. Nothing in this input is predictable from English statistics; the only
usable signal is the repetition itself. A head that has learned induction should
light up along a single off-diagonal at offset `seqLen - 1 = 14`, because from
position `i` in the second copy, the token that followed the match sits exactly
14 places back.

Scoring is then just: average that diagonal.

```python
diag = pattern[head].diagonal(offset=-(SEQ - 1))
score = diag.mean().item()
```

Run it over all 12 layers × 12 heads and sort.

The top six heads by induction score:

| head  | induction score |
| ----- | --------------- |
| L5H5  | 0.828           |
| L6H9  | 0.793           |
| L5H1  | 0.785           |
| L7H10 | 0.772           |
| L7H2  | 0.611           |
| L10H7 | 0.576           |

Mean across all 144 heads: **0.117**.

So the claim holds, with one wrinkle. L5H5, L5H1 and L6H9 are all in the top
three by _set_, but the ordering isn't what the folklore suggests — L6H9 edges out
L5H1 here. That ordering is not stable anyway: it shifts with the random seed and
the sequence length, because the score is an average over a short diagonal. The
robust finding is the **gap**, not the ranking. The top heads sit around 7× the
mean head, and there is a visible cliff after the first four.

Worth naming what this probe does _not_ show. A high score on a repeated-random
sequence means a head attends in the induction pattern — not that it is used for
induction on natural text, and not that ablating it would hurt in-context
learning. Those are separate experiments with separate claims.

## Why the heatmap is precomputed

Every number above was generated once by
[`scripts/figures/gpt2_induction_attention.py`](https://github.com/taufique74/taufique74.github.io/blob/main/scripts/figures/gpt2_induction_attention.py)
and committed as a 32 KB JSON file. The page ships a canvas renderer and that
file — no model runs in your browser, and nothing runs on a server.

That is a deliberate constraint rather than a limitation I ran into. Keeping the
data layer static means the interactive parts cost a few kilobytes instead of a
few hundred megabytes, the figures stay reproducible from a script in the repo,
and the whole site remains hostable anywhere that can serve files.
